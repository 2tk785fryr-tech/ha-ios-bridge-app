## The idea

(Michael, 30 Oct.) The app works **without Home Assistant** for people who don't use it, with
**Apple Home (HomeKit) or Google Home** standing in for as much of what Home Assistant does as
possible. **Home Assistant stays the default and recommended setup.** Plus **mixed commutes with
trains**.

1. **Lights and the house follow the alarms**, as Home Assistant does today: the lights fade up on
   snooze, come on when the alarm is stopped, and everything else it does with lights depending on
   what you do with the alarms.
2. **The backstops still fire**: the home's hub (not the phone) turns lights on at the backstop
   times, so they work even if the phone is dead or the app never runs.
3. **Whatever else can be carried over**, to go through one by one (list below).
4. **Shortcuts**: more detailed instructions, and basic shortcuts Michael can share, so someone
   without Home Assistant keeps as much as they can.
5. **Mixed commutes**: walk or drive to a station, a **target train**, then walk to work. Show the
   **next train** after the target and what the commute (and arrival) looks like if you miss it.
   Examples: Japan (walk → a certain train → walk to the ship); DC (drive to the station → a
   certain train → a short walk).

It loses much of what makes the app automatic, so this needs planning before any items.

## What's possible (research notes, 30 Oct; to confirm before building)

**Apple Home (HomeKit).** The app can use HomeKit directly (the HomeKit entitlement; paid account,
which Michael has):
- **Control** lights, scenes, switches, locks and **security systems** (arm / disarm) from the app,
  including from snooze and stop, which already run the app in the background. No shortcut or
  phone command needed.
- **Fade:** HomeKit has no "fade over 10 minutes" command. Options: step the brightness from the
  phone while the snooze runs (only while the app is allowed to run), or have the app write a set
  of hub automations a minute apart (works with the phone asleep). To decide.
- **Backstops by the hub:** the app can **create and update Home automations** (timer triggers
  with scenes) each night for the backstop times. A home hub (Apple TV or HomePod) runs them even
  with the phone off. This is the closest match to Home Assistant's ladder. The app would cancel
  them when you're up (stop, door opened, etc.).
- **Read sensors:** door, motion and occupancy sensors in Home. The app can read them when it runs,
  and Home automations can react to them on the hub ("bedroom door opens → turn on hall light"), but
  the app can't be woken by a sensor change. So "Stop keeps the rest until you're up" and the
  no-alarm morning flow need a different trigger (a Home automation running a shortcut, or the
  phone checking when an alarm rings).
- **Presence:** the app can use its own location (geofence) for "only when you're home" instead of
  Home Assistant's person entity.

**Google Home.** Google's Home APIs have an iOS SDK for controlling devices and creating
automations, but it needs a Google developer project, OAuth sign-in and Google's approval to ship
beyond testers. Possible, but a much bigger setup than HomeKit. To decide whether it's in 0.5.0 or
later, or done through Shortcuts and the Google Home app only.

**What the app can already do alone** (no smart home at all): plan, alarms, snooze/stop, drive
times (Apple Maps, TomTom, Google), weather (Apple Weather, Open-Meteo), leave reminders (phone
only, no re-checks while asleep), Live Activities (updated by the phone, not by push), nap,
wind-down notifications (from the phone), the weekly summary (kept on the phone).

**What's lost without Home Assistant** (unless a Home automation or shortcut covers it): pushes to
the phone while the app is asleep (Live Activity pushes, re-checks at leave time, Up for the day?),
bed sensors and "in bed" detection, the missing-plan nudge, the shift override and `current_shift`
cross-check, and backstops beyond lights (announcements, speaker alarms).

**Trains.** Apple Maps gives only a transit travel time, not trains or departure times. Google's
Routes API gives transit routes with the line, departure and arrival times, and later departures,
which is what a target train and the next train need. It doesn't plan "drive to the station, then
train" in one go, so the app would plan the legs separately: walk or drive to the station, the
train, walk from the station. Google's transit data covers Japan and DC.

## Areas to plan (each becomes items once decided)

1. **Choosing the setup:** Home Assistant (default), Apple Home, Google Home, or none, in
   onboarding and Settings. The app already talks to Home Assistant through one "link"; Apple Home
   would be another link doing the same jobs.
2. **Alarm → house actions in Apple Home:** for each moment (snooze, stop, all alarms ended, back
   to bed, temporary alarm stopped, nap start / end, wind-down, bedtime), pick a scene or devices.
   Defaults that mirror today's Home Assistant setup (Wake Snooze Fade, Wake Sequence).
3. **Backstops on the hub:** which rungs, what each does (lights on, a scene, security system), how
   they're created, moved and cancelled each night, and what happens with no hub.
4. **Up / in bed without Home Assistant:** what stands in for the bed sensor and the bedroom door
   (a Home sensor checked when an alarm rings, a Home automation running a shortcut, or nothing).
5. **Each Home Assistant feature, one by one:** carry over, partly, or not (the list above).
6. **Shortcuts kit:** the shortcuts to share (signed, in Desktop → Shortcuts → to import), with a
   step-by-step guide for a non-Home-Assistant setup.
7. **Mixed commutes:** legs (walk / drive / train / walk), the target train, the next train and the
   arrival if you miss it, how it shows on Your morning, the widget and the Live Activity, and how
   leave-by and the backstops follow the target train.

## Decided (30 Oct)

- **Apple Home first.** Google Home later (not in 0.5.0).
- **Fade by hub automations**, stepping the brightness up a minute apart, so the phone can be
  asleep.
- **One home system, not a mix:** Home Assistant, Apple Home, or none. Someone on Home Assistant
  uses HomeKit at most for Siri and the Home app (as Michael does), and that stays untouched.
- **Trains from Google** (Routes API, with a key); Apple Maps (or the chosen service) for the walk
  and drive legs.
- **Target train per event** that needs a train, with a **default per shift type**. The shift's
  default can't be one fixed train, because the time you have to be at work changes (Tuesdays on
  Swings). Proposal: the shift's default is a rule, *"the latest train that gets me there N min
  before the shift starts"*, worked out from that day's shift start, so a different Tuesday start
  picks a different train by itself. The event (or that night) can still pick another train.
  **Agreed.**
- **Stations found from the address** (Google's transit route picks the boarding and getting-off
  stations from home and the event's location), probably; a way to pin a station if it picks the
  wrong one is to be decided.
- **Time at the station:** 5 min by default (parking, walking to the platform), adjustable.
- **The next train shows everywhere the commute does:** Your morning, the widget and the Live
  Activity (e.g. "6:12 train · next 6:24, arrive 7:05").

## Decided (31 Oct)

- **Choosing the setup:** asked at onboarding (Home Assistant pre-selected) and changeable in
  Settings.
- **Alarm → house actions in Apple Home:** one Home **scene per moment** (snooze, stop, all alarms
  ended, back to bed, temporary alarm stopped, nap start / end, wind-down, bedtime). The app offers
  to create default scenes that mirror today's setup (Wake Snooze Fade, Wake Sequence).
- **Backstop rungs** can turn on lights or run a scene, and change the security system (disarm or
  a mode), and also open curtains or blinds, turn switches and outlets on or off (a fan, a coffee
  maker), and set the thermostat. A HomePod alarm sound isn't possible
  from the app; it would be a Home app automation the person makes, with instructions.
- **Shortcuts:** detailed step-by-step instructions, plus a link to slimmed-down importable
  shortcuts that help the person set it up.
- **Up without a bed sensor:** both. A chosen Home sensor (door, motion, occupancy) is checked
  when an alarm rings or is stopped; with no sensor, or one that can't tell, a notification
  after Stop asks "Up?" and the rest of the alarms stay until it's answered.
- **Rebuilt without Home Assistant, from the phone:** the leave-time drive re-check, the
  missing-plan nudge (an evening notification when no alarms are set), Live Activity updates
  (whenever the phone runs), and Good night running the bedtime scene directly.
- **A late or cancelled target train:** the app tells you, with suggestions (move the alarms
  or leave-by to catch the next train), and changes nothing until you pick one.
  It's checked when alarms are set, when the first alarm rings, and whenever the app opens before
  leave-by. The suggestions come as a notification with actions ("Move alarms", "Take the 6:24")
  and the same on Your morning.
- **The shortcuts kit:** Bedtime (a slimmed-down I'm Ready For Bed: plan alarms, the bedtime
  scene), Good morning (the morning scene once the alarms end), a Setup helper (walks through
  making the Home scenes and automations), and Leave check (re-checks the drive or train and
  reminds you).
- **Pinning a station:** per shift type, in that shift's commute settings (boarding and getting-off
  station); an event can override it.
- **The Google Routes key:** Michael's own key is built in for testing with a few people. It's
  restricted to the app's bundle ID, with a daily quota cap in Google Cloud so it can't run up a
  bill. A rough estimate: a handful of testers checking a few times a night and morning is a
  couple of thousand calls a month, which should sit inside Google's free monthly calls (to
  confirm on Google's pricing page before building). Anyone beyond that enters their own key in
  Settings, as for the drive services today, with detailed step-by-step directions for getting
  one. The built-in key can be turned off in a later release.
- **Switching home systems:** each system's settings are kept; switching only changes which one
  is used.
- **Carried over to Apple Home:** a wind-down scene (a hub automation at wind-down time); nap
  start and end each run a scene (Nap Focus stays on the phone); the weekly summary, kept on the
  phone; and bedtime away reading Home's locks, doors and security system.
- **No home system at all ("none") is supported:** phone only, with alarms, the plan, drives,
  trains, weather and reminders, and no house actions or hub backstops.
- **No home hub:** setup warns that backstops need an Apple TV or HomePod; without one, only the
  phone's own alarms act as backstops.

## Items

Drafted 31 Oct from the decisions above. Built in this order: 1–2 first (everything else hangs on
them), then Apple Home (3–9), phone-only rebuilds (10), trains (11–14), shortcuts and docs (15).
Home Assistant setups must behave exactly as in 0.4.9 throughout.

1. **Home system choice.** Onboarding's first question: Home Assistant (pre-selected,
   "recommended"), Apple Home, or None (phone only). Settings → Home system changes it later.
   Each system's settings are kept when you switch; only the active one is used. The app's
   existing Home Assistant "link" becomes one of three links (Home Assistant, Apple Home, none)
   behind the same jobs: report a snooze/stop/nap/bedtime, arm and cancel backstops, is the
   person up, is the house all right. Test: switching keeps both sets of settings; Home
   Assistant reports unchanged.

2. **Phone-only (None).** Everything the app already does alone keeps working with no link:
   plan, alarms, snooze/stop, drives, weather, leave reminders, nap, wind-down notifications,
   the weekly summary on the phone, Live Activities updated by the phone. Screens that only make
   sense with a home system (backstops, house status, phone commands, Bedtime pending) are
   hidden, not shown empty. Test: a full evening → morning with None sends nothing anywhere.

3. **Apple Home access.** The HomeKit entitlement and permission, asked when Apple Home is
   chosen. Pick the home. Setup checks for a home hub (Apple TV or HomePod); with none it warns
   that backstops will be phone-only, and continues. Test: no-hub warning; permission refused
   explains and falls back to None.

4. **A scene per moment.** Settings → Apple Home → Scenes: one Home scene for each of snooze,
   stop, all alarms ended, back to bed, temporary alarm stopped, nap start, nap end, wind-down
   and bedtime (any can be left empty). "Create default scenes" makes ones that mirror today's
   Home Assistant setup (Wake Sequence, Good Night, …), which the person then fills in the Home
   app. The app runs the chosen scene from the alarm buttons, the Stop screen, Good night and the
   nap. Test: each moment runs its scene; an empty one runs nothing.

5. **The snooze fade by the hub.** On snooze, the app writes Home timer automations a minute
   apart that step the chosen lights' brightness up over the snooze, and removes them when the
   alarm is stopped or the fade finishes, so it works with the phone asleep. Test: the steps
   written for a 9-minute snooze; removed on stop.

6. **Backstops on the hub.** Each night, when alarms are set, the app writes one Home timer
   automation per rung (early, deadline, late, from the same times as today's ladder). A rung can
   run a scene or set lights, change the security system (disarm or a mode), open curtains or
   blinds, turn switches and outlets on or off, and set the thermostat. They're moved when the
   alarms change and removed when you're up, the alarms are cancelled, or a no-alarm morning.
   No hub: none written; the phone's own alarms are the backstop. Test: written, moved, removed;
   a stop that isn't "up" leaves them.

7. **Up without a bed sensor.** Settings → Apple Home → "How I'm up": an optional Home sensor
   (door, motion or occupancy), read when an alarm rings or is stopped. With no sensor, or one
   that can't tell, a notification after Stop asks "Up?" (Up / Still in bed), and the rest of
   this wake-up's alarms and the backstops stay until it's answered. Drives keep-until-up, the
   no-alarm morning and removing the backstops. Test: sensor says up; no sensor asks; unanswered
   keeps everything.

8. **Wind-down, nap and away in Apple Home.** Wind-down runs its scene from a hub automation at
   wind-down time. Nap start and end run their scenes (Nap Focus stays on the phone as today).
   Bedtime away reads Home's locks, doors and security system for "home is all right". Test:
   each from a fake home.

9. **Good night in Apple Home.** Good night (the bar's slide-and-hold and Siri) runs the bedtime
   scene directly instead of asking Home Assistant to push RUN_BEDTIME. Test: runs the scene once
   per night; "already ran" after.

10. **Rebuilt from the phone (Apple Home and None).** The leave-time drive re-check scheduled by
    the phone (a background refresh near leave-by; said plainly that it may not run if the phone
    is asleep), the missing-plan nudge (an evening notification when no alarms are set for a
    work morning), and Live Activity updates whenever the app runs. Test: each scheduled at the
    right time; nothing sent to Home Assistant.

11. **Train legs.** A commute can be walk or drive to a station → a train → walk to work. Trains
    and stations come from Google's Routes API (transit); the walk and drive legs from Apple Maps
    or the chosen drive service. 5 min at the station by default (parking, the platform),
    adjustable. Stations are found from home and the event's address; each shift type can pin
    the boarding and getting-off station, and an event can override it. Test: legs added up;
    a pinned station used over Google's pick.

12. **The target train.** Per shift type, a rule: "the latest train that gets me there N min
    before the shift starts", worked out from that day's start (so a different Tuesday start
    picks a different train). An event, or that night, can pick another train. Leave-by, the
    alarms and the backstops follow the target train. Test: two shift starts pick two trains; an
    event override wins.

13. **The next train everywhere.** Your morning, the widget and the Live Activity show the target
    and the next one after it, and the arrival if you miss it: "6:12 train · next 6:24, arrive
    7:05". Test: text for each size and the Live Activity.

14. **A late or cancelled train.** Checked when alarms are set, when the first alarm rings, and
    whenever the app opens before leave-by. If the target is late or cancelled, a notification
    with actions ("Move alarms", "Take the 6:24") and the same on Your morning; nothing changes
    until you pick one. Test: late and cancelled each suggest; nothing moves untouched.

15. **The Google key, shortcuts kit and docs.**
    - **Key:** Michael's key built in for testers, restricted to the app's bundle ID with a daily
      quota cap; Settings takes your own key, with step-by-step directions to get one. Confirm
      Google's free monthly calls cover testing before building.
    - **Shortcuts kit:** Bedtime (a slimmed I'm Ready For Bed), Good morning, a Setup helper (walks
      through making the Home scenes and automations) and Leave check, shared by link, with
      detailed instructions in `docs/shortcuts.md`.
    - **Docs:** `docs/setup.md` gets an Apple Home path and a phone-only path beside Home
      Assistant; the README says Home Assistant is recommended and what each setup loses.
